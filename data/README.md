# The dataset

> **Everything in this directory is synthetic. It is generated by
> `generate.py` and represents no real company, customer, employee,
> or transaction. Company names are assembled from a word list in
> the generator. Person names come from Faker. Any resemblance to a
> real organization is a coincidence produced by a random seed.**

I built this dataset because I wanted somewhere to write RevOps SQL
that behaved like a CRM export rather than like a textbook. That
means the numbers are plausible, the relationships hold, and a
meaningful share of the rows are wrong on purpose.

## Regenerating it

```
python generate.py     # writes CSVs into data/csv/
```

Or, from the repo root, `python setup.py` runs the generator and
loads the result into `pipeline.duckdb`.

The seed is **4207**, set at the top of `generate.py`. Same seed,
same database, every time. `data/csv/` is gitignored because it is
a build artifact: it is reproduced exactly by running the script.

## What is in it

| Table | Rows | Grain |
|---|---:|---|
| `territories` | 8 | One row per sales territory |
| `reps` | 25 | One row per seller, current and departed |
| `accounts` | 306 | One row per account record (300 companies, 6 duplicates) |
| `leads` | 4,000 | One row per inbound or sourced lead |
| `opportunities` | 2,546 | One row per deal |
| `stage_history` | 6,563 | One row per recorded stage change or ownership change |
| `activities` | 36,897 | One row per logged call, email, meeting, demo or note |

The window runs **2024-01-01 through 2025-12-31**, and the dataset
is frozen at that end date. Queries that need a notion of "today"
use `DATE '2025-12-31'` as a literal rather than `current_date`, so
results do not drift with the wall clock.

Stage order is `Prospecting > Discovery > Proposal > Negotiation >
Closed Won | Closed Lost`.

## What is realistic on purpose

These are modeled in, so the queries have something real to find:

- **Rep ramp.** New hires run at roughly 20 percent of full
  productivity in month one, about 60 percent by month three, full
  by month six. Ramp affects both how many deals a rep is assigned
  and how well those deals convert.
- **A slower Q3 cohort.** Reps hired in July, August or September
  walk the same ramp curve at a little over half speed. The premise
  is that they onboard through the summer slowdown and then hit Q4,
  when their manager is closing rather than coaching.
- **Attrition and reassignment.** Eight of the 25 reps have a
  termination date, weighted toward lower performers. Their open
  deals are reassigned, recorded in `stage_history` as a row where
  `from_stage` equals `to_stage` and `changed_by_rep_id` is the new
  owner. A handful are deliberately left with the departed rep.
- **Source quality that differs at both steps.** Referral converts
  best and is scarce. Inbound converts well and is capacity
  limited. Outbound is high volume and low yield. Paid has the
  highest cost per lead. The differences show up in lead to
  opportunity rate, in win rate, and in cycle length.
- **Seasonality.** Q4 closes better, July and August close worse,
  and lead volume follows the same curve.
- **A growth trend.** Lead volume rises about 50 percent across the
  window, which is why open pipeline at the as of date is larger
  than a flat run rate would leave behind.
- **Forecast optimism.** Deals get called Commit more often than
  they deserve, and the gap is widest for reps who have been there
  the least time.

None of that landed on the first try. `../notes/BUILDING_THE_DATA.md`
is the log of the four rounds of diagnosis it took, including the
parameters that moved each time, so the failures can be reproduced
by reverting them.

## Deliberate imperfections

This is the part that makes the dataset worth querying.
`queries/12_data_hygiene_audit.sql` finds all of it. Counts are
from the seeded build.

| What is wrong | Rows | Why it is in here |
|---|---:|---|
| Opportunity has no `amount` | 127 | Reps create records before they have a number. Every revenue total in the repo has to decide what to do about it. |
| `amount` is zero or negative | 12 | Bad imports and manual corrections. These quietly subtract from real revenue. |
| `actual_close_date` before `created_date` | 30 | Backdated renewals and migration errors. Produces negative sales cycles. |
| Closed stage, no close date | 25 | Invisible to any report filtered by close month. |
| Open stage, close date populated | 15 | The record counts as open pipeline and as closed revenue depending on the filter. |
| Closed Lost with no `loss_reason` | 167 | Not a reporting break, a learning break. |
| `is_won` disagrees with `stage` | 27 | Two columns, two answers, one argument in a pipeline meeting. |
| Open deal, no stage movement in 180 days | 19 | Nobody closed it out. Inflates every coverage ratio. |
| Near duplicate account names | 12 | Six pairs, differing only by punctuation, case, or legal suffix. |
| Lead marked converted with nothing linked | 25 | Marketing attribution loses these deals entirely. |
| `stage_history` rows that skip a stage | 25 | Reps jump Discovery. The funnel undercounts it. |
| Opportunity with zero logged activities | 76 | Either it was never worked or the rep does not log. |
| Open deal owned by a departed rep | 5 | Live pipeline nobody is working, still counted in coverage. |

None of this is cleaned before loading. The database gets the mess,
and the SQL deals with it, which is the arrangement you get in real
life.

## Conventions worth knowing before you read the queries

- **`stage` is authoritative, not `is_won`.** The two disagree on 27
  records. The queries define a win as `stage = 'Closed Won'` and
  query 12 counts the disagreement rather than hiding it.
- **Reassignment rows are not stage movement.** Any query that
  measures velocity filters `from_stage <> to_stage`.
- **`opportunities.source` is denormalized from the originating
  lead.** It is copied at conversion, so queries join back to
  `leads` when cost matters and use the local column when only the
  label matters.
- **No foreign keys are declared.** A CRM export has no enforced
  referential integrity, and declaring the constraints would let the
  loader reject the rows that make this interesting. Integrity is
  checked in SQL instead.
